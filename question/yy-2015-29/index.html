For years, studies have found that first-generation college students – those who do not have a parent with a college degree – lag other students on a range of education achievement factors. Their grades are lower and their dropout rates are higher. But since such students are most likely to advance economically if they succeed in higher education, colleges and universities have pushed for decades to recruit more of them. This has created “a paradox” in that recruiting first-generation students, but then watching many of them fail, means that higher education has “continued to reproduce and widen, rather than close” an achievement gap based on social class, according to the depressing beginning of a paper forthcoming in the journal Psychological Science.

But the article is actually quite optimistic, as it outlines a potential solution to this problem, suggesting that an approach (which involves a one-hour, next-to-no-cost program) can close 63 percent of the achievement gap (measured by such factors as grades) between first-generation and other students.

The authors of the paper are from different universities, and their findings are based on a study involving 147 students (who completed the project) at an unnamed private university. First generation was defined as not having a parent with a four-year college degree. Most of the first-generation students (59.1 percent) were recipients of Pell Grants, a federal grant for undergraduates with financial need, while this was true only for 8.6 percent of the students with at least one parent with a four-year degree.

Their thesis – that a relatively modest intervention could have a big impact – was based on the view that first-generation students may be most lacking not in potential but in practical knowledge about how to deal with the issues that face most college students. They cite past research by several authors to show that this is the gap that must be narrowed to close the achievement gap.

Many first-generation students “struggle to navigate the middle-class culture of higher education, learn the ‘rules of the game,’ and take advantage of college resources,” they write. And this becomes more of a problem when colleges don’t talk about the class advantages and disadvantages of different groups of students.

“Because US colleges and universities seldom acknowledge how social class can affect students’ educational experiences, many first-generation students lack insight about why they are struggling and do not understand how students ‘like them’ can improve.”

本题讲解：2015 阅读第 29 题 · "not in potential but in practical knowledge"——not...but... 取舍结构锁定缺什么

答案：B（are inexperienced in handling their issues at college 在处理在校问题时缺乏经验）。

第 0 步：先立机制——观点题先找观点句，再看取舍结构的重心

题干问论文作者 believe first-generation students 怎么样。观点句在第四段①句，其核心是一个 not...but...（不是……而是……）的取舍结构：第一代学生最缺乏的，不是潜力（potential），而是处理大多数大学生都会面对的问题的实用知识（practical knowledge about how to deal with the issues that face most college students）。not...but... 的语义重心永远落在 but 之后——"缺什么"的答案就是 practical knowledge 那半句。把它翻译成选项语言：缺乏处理在校问题的"实用知识"＝在处理在校问题方面缺乏经验。

第 1 步：定位原文

Their thesis – that a relatively modest intervention could have a big impact – was based on the view that first-generation students may be most lacking not in potential but in practical knowledge about how to deal with the issues that face most college students.
  • 缺什么（not）：potential 潜力——原文明确说不缺这个；
  • 缺什么（but）：practical knowledge about how to deal with the issues——处理在校实际问题的知识。

第 2 步：对号入座

选项词义判定理由
A may lack opportunities to apply for research projects缺少申请研究项目的机会✗ 排除lacking 与 past research 两个词被糅在一起；"研究项目"在原文无处安放，无中生有
B are inexperienced in handling their issues at college在处理在校问题时缺乏经验✓ 当选lacking practical knowledge about how to deal with the issues ＝ 处理在校问题没经验——同义概括
C can have a potential influence on other students对其他学生有潜在影响✗ 排除potential 一词被挪用：原文说"不缺潜力"，不是"对他人有潜在影响"——词义偷换
D are actually indifferent to the achievement gap对成就差距漠不关心✗ 排除achievement gap 被挪用；末段反而暗示他们渴望进步，indifferent 无文本支撑

第 3 步：预判追问

  • practical knowledge 怎么变成 inexperience？：knowledge about how to deal with...（关于"如何应对"的知识）落在行为层面就是"会不会处理、熟不熟练"。缺乏这类知识＝处理起来生疏＝inexperienced——这是"知识"到"经验"的同层改写，不是越级推断。
  • 为什么 C 只错在一个词也要排除？：potential 在原文的搭配是 lacking not in potential（不缺潜力）。C 把这个词从"学生自身的潜力"偷换成"对他人的潜在影响"，搭配对象整个换了——观点题里关键词的搭配位置与词义同等重要。

易错点

  • 误选 A（research projects）：被 lacking 和 past research 的字面捕获，"申请研究项目"原文零支撑。
  • 误选 C（potential influence）：认识 potential 这个词就往选项上靠，没核对它在原文的搭配（不缺潜力）。
  • 误选 D（indifferent）：被末段 achievement gap 捕获；文章基调是他们渴求方法而不得，谈不上"漠不关心"。
记忆锚点：not...but... 的重心在 but 之后——缺的是 practical knowledge（处理在校问题的实用知识）＝inexperienced；干扰项常拿原文词汇换搭配：potential（不缺潜力→对他人影响）、achievement gap（问题描述→个人态度）。
