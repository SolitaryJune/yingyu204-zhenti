Read the following four texts. Answer the questions after each text by choosing A, B, C or D. Mark your answers on the ANSWER SHEET.

A century ago, the immigrants from across the Atlantic included settlers and sojourners. Along with the many folks looking to make a permanent home in the United States came those who had no intention to stay, and who would make some money and then go home. Between 1908 and 1915, about 7 million people arrived while about 2 million departed. About a quarter of all Italian immigrants, for example, eventually returned to Italy for good. They even had an affectionate nickname, “uccelli di passaggio,” birds of passage.

Today, we are much more rigid about immigrants. We divide newcomers into two categories: legal or illegal, good or bad. We hail them as Americans in the making, or brand them as aliens to be kicked out. That framework has contributed mightily to our broken immigration system and the long political paralysis over how to fix it. We don’t need more categories, but we need to change the way we think about categories. We need to look beyond strict definitions of legal and illegal. To start, we can recognize the new birds of passage, those living and thriving in the gray areas. We might then begin to solve our immigration challenges.

Crop pickers, violinists, construction workers, entrepreneurs, engineers, home health-care aides and physicists are among today’s birds of passage. They are energetic participants in a global economy driven by the flow of work, money and ideas. They prefer to come and go as opportunity calls them. They can manage to have a job in one place and a family in another.

With or without permission, they straddle laws, jurisdictions and identities with ease. We need them to imagine the United States as a place where they can be productive for a while without committing themselves to staying forever. We need them to feel that home can be both here and there and that they can belong to two nations honorably.

Accommodating this new world of people in motion will require new attitudes on both sides of the immigration battle. Looking beyond the culture war logic of right or wrong means opening up the middle ground and understanding that managing immigration today requires multiple paths and multiple outcomes, including some that are not easy to accomplish legally in the existing system.

本题讲解：2013 阅读第 30 题 · 全文最恰当的标题

答案：D（Legal or Illegal: Big Mistake “合法还是非法”：大错特错）。

第 0 步：先立机制——批评型文章的标题 = 批评对象 + 态度

本文不是中立说明文，而是批评文：作者点名抨击现行移民体制的思维方式。批评型文章的标题公式是“批评对象 + 态度”。所以解题分两步：① 找到作者点名批评的对象（注意 that framework 这类指代词的指向）；② 核对标题前半与后半是否分别对齐“对象”与“态度”。

第 1 步：定位批评句

We divide newcomers into two categories: legal or illegal, good or bad... That framework has contributed mightily to our broken immigration system and the long political paralysis over how to fix it.

作者点名批评的正是“legal or illegal”这套二分框架——它“极大地促成”了制度失灵与政治僵局。后文又呼吁 look beyond strict definitions of legal and illegal。批评对象 = legal or illegal；态度 = 这套思维大错特错。

第 2 步：逐项排除

选项判定理由与典型误解
A Come and Go: Big Mistake✗ 排除批评对象错位：来去自由（come and go）是作者支持的东西，他反对的是“不许来去自由”的现行体制——说“来去”是大错与作者立场相反。（典型误解：只抓表面话题词，不核对“谁被批评”。）
B Living and Thriving: Great Risk✗ 排除原词设障：living and thriving 确实出自第二段（灰色地带里生活发展），但作者说这是应该被“承认”的正面状态，没说有“巨大风险”；且这只是细节，非主旨。（典型误解：原文词重现 = 高危选项。）
C With or Without: Great Risk✗ 排除含义模糊：“有或没有”（候鸟？）与文章内容建立不起明确联系，勉强解释也大大偏离主旨。（典型误解：无实际对应，纯干扰。）
D Legal or Illegal: Big Mistake✓ 当选前半 = 现行体制（二分法），后半 = 态度（作者认为它是错误）——对象与态度双双对齐，罩住全文。

第 3 步：预判追问

  • 标题能罩住全文吗？：能。第一段引子讲“百年前对旅居者的宽容”，是给批评提供对照背景；第二段点名批评“合法 / 非法”二分法；三至五段论述“错在哪（逼僵化分类）、怎么办（承认候鸟、打开中间地带）”。标题的“Big Mistake”正是对第 2 段批评句与全文呼吁的浓缩。
  • 为什么 B 的原词反而危险？：出题人从原文里摘词（living and thriving）再造一个“听起来有哲理”的标题，但态度（Great Risk）是凭空安的——文中没有任何“风险”论述。判断标题先核对态度词有没有出处。

易错点

  • 误选 A：把作者支持的东西（来去自由）当成了被批评的对象。
  • 误选 B：被原词吸引，没发现“Great Risk”在文中无出处。
记忆锚点：批评型文章标题 = 批评对象 + 态度词；that framework = legal or illegal 二分法；对象错位（A）、原词 + 凭空态度（B）、无对应（C）都罩不住全文。
